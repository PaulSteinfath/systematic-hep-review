  • Studies: Unique publications (identified by PMID). Each study represents one research paper.

Results

Overview of the included studies

In total, 132 studies published between 1986 and 2024 were included in the review. The vast majority of studies used EEG recordings (123 studies, 93.2%), while only 9 studies (6.8%) employed MEG to measure HERs.

Study type distribution:

  • Task-based: 92 studies (69.7%)
  • Resting-state: 33 studies (25%)
  • Both: 7 studies (5.3%)

Reference Schemes

For the data acquisition, we can observe some variability with respect to the used online reference. This variability is reduced during data processing, where the dominant choice is Common Average Reference (CAR, used in 46.3% of the studies).

Artifact Removal

Artifact Removal Statistics

In terms of artifact reduction, many studies reported the removal of noisy epochs (55%) and bad channels (16%). Independent Component Analysis (ICA) is by far the most commonly used technique used by 73.5% of all studies to remove artifacts related to cardiac, eye movement, muscle or other nuisance sources.

Filter Settings

When analysing relatively slow evoked responses, like the HER, optimal filter settings are an important consideration to not remove or induce effects. We observe that across studies, filtering is most frequently done between 0.5 Hz and 30 Hz, with the most dominant high-pass filter cutoffs at 0.5, 0.1, 1 Hz (Fig. 3E).

Peak Effect Distribution

For cluster-based analysis, the peak in the distribution where most effects are found occurs at 250 ms (R-peak) and 307 ms (T-wave, corresponding to approximately 607 ms after the R-peak). For averaging, the peak occurs at 490 ms (R-peak) and 234 ms (T-wave, corresponding to approximately 534 ms after the R-peak).

Statistical Comparison of Peak Effect Timings

** Clustering vs Averaging - R-peak : - Sample sizes: n₁ = 106 , n₂ = 114 - Median timings: 323.0 ms vs 441.5 ms - Median difference: -118.5 ms - Mann-Whitney U test: W = 4180 , p < 0.001 - Effect size: r = 0.951 ( large ) - Conclusion: Peak effect timings are significantly different** between methods

** Clustering vs Averaging - T-peak : - Sample sizes: n₁ = 11 , n₂ = 12 - Median timings: 270.5 ms vs 270.5 ms - Median difference: 0.0 ms - Mann-Whitney U test: W = 66 , p = 1.000 - Effect size: r = 0.000 ( negligible ) - Conclusion**: Peak effect timings are not significantly different between methods

** Clustering vs Averaging - Combined (R-peak equivalent) : - Sample sizes: n₁ = 117 , n₂ = 126 - Median timings: 348.0 ms vs 451.5 ms - Median difference: -103.5 ms - Mann-Whitney U test: W = 5431 , p < 0.001 - Effect size: r = 0.796 ( large ) - Conclusion: Peak effect timings are significantly different** between methods

Interpretation

The statistical analysis reveals significant differences in peak effect timings between clustering and averaging methods, with clustering effects occurring on average 103.5 ms earlier than averaging effects.

This suggests that the choice of analysis method (clustering vs averaging) systematically affects the temporal localization of significant HEP effects, which has important implications for interpreting and comparing results across studies.
